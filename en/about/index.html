About this course

Quantum Mechanics — finite systems, continuous systems and foundations.

What is here

The course has three parts, 11 chapters and 44 lectures, with 100 figures. The site's lecture text comes from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes. The site checks theorem, equation and figure numbers against the typeset volume. Each lecture has its own PDF.

Each lecture opens with its objectives and the outcomes it aims at. Tick an outcome when you can do it; its arrow takes you down to the section that develops it. The ticks are kept in this browser and counted on the My progress page.

The three parts

Part I, Finite Systems, asks four questions of a system: what are its states, what are its observables, how does it evolve, and what does a measurement give? The answers start from postulates, and in finite dimension everything that follows from them can be proved. It goes from the qubit to systems with n levels and to several systems together.

Part II, Continuous Systems, asks the same questions of a particle in a box, on a line and in space, where position and momentum are unbounded operators and spectral measures give the probabilities of readings.

Part III, Foundations, examines the structure of the rules that Parts I and II used, and the assumptions behind them: states, symmetry, parts and open systems, measurement, and the measurement problem.

The course is written for reading alone, by students who have taken Linear Algebra for Physics.

How much is proved

The notes are honest about rigour. A result with no tag is proved in full; in Parts II and III a proof may use the stated standing facts. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used, and its proof lives in the references. In Parts I and II, computer-algebra scripts check selected calculations the notes print.

Practice and review

The lectures have self-checks, and the exercise sheets have hints and answers. The Review page collects practice material. Part I also has interactive labs.

Found a mistake?

Please report it to the course's author. Each correction is fixed at source and republished.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source and checked against the typeset volume; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the style file the PDFs load; the diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF, converted to SVG. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
