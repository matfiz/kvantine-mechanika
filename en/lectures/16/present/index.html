Lecture 16

The particle in a box

A chain with fixed ends suggests the energy rule for a particle in a box. We build the self-adjoint Hamiltonian from the sine basis and prove that its energy postulate determines it uniquely. Energy readings remain defined for states outside its domain. Its evolution is Lecture 17’s subject.

16.2 Wavefunctions in a box

The chain’s state was a vector of amplitudes, one for each site. A state of the particle assigns an amplitude ψ(x)\psi(x)ψ(x) to each position xxx in the box. The function ψ\psiψ is the wavefunction of the state. Lecture 17 postulates that ∣ψ(x)∣2\abs{\psi(x)}^2∣ψ(x)∣2 is the probability density for finding the particle near xxx. The wavefunctions form a vector space with the inner product ⟨φ,ψ⟩=∫0Lφ(x)‾ ψ(x) dx,\begin{equation} \label{eq:16:inner} \inner\varphi\psi=\int_0^L\overline{\varphi(x)}\,\psi(x)\,\dd x , \end{equation}⟨φ,ψ⟩=∫0L​φ(x)​ψ(x)dx,(16.3) which is antilinear in the first slot, as in Part I. The space is L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L). Its elements are the functions with ∫0L∣ψ∣2dx<∞\int_0^L\abs{\psi}^2\dd x<\infty∫0L​∣ψ∣2dx<∞, and two functions that differ only on a set of measure zero count as the same element (Linear Algebra, Def. 13.7). With the norm ∥ψ∥=⟨ψ,ψ⟩1/2\norm\psi=\inner\psi\psi^{1/2}∥ψ∥=⟨ψ,ψ⟩1/2, the space L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L) is a Hilbert space, a complete inner product space.

So an element of L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L) has no value at a single point. Changing ψ\psiψ at x=0x=0x=0 alone changes no integral, and it does not change the element. A condition such as ψ(0)=0\psi(0)=0ψ(0)=0 therefore cannot be imposed on the states. It will return as a condition on the domain of the Hamiltonian.

16.3 Stationary waves

The chain of §16.1 suggests Schrödinger’s rule: for a free particle of mass mmm, the energy acts on a wavefunction by ψ↦−(ℏ2/2m) ψ′′\psi\mapsto-(\hbar^2/2m)\,\psi''ψ↦−(ℏ2/2m)ψ′′. We take the rule as given here, and §16.5 makes it a postulate. A wavefunction of definite energy EEE, a stationary wave, solves −(ℏ2/2m) u′′=Eu-(\hbar^2/2m)\,u''=Eu−(ℏ2/2m)u′′=Eu. The physics

In a cyanine dye, a chain of carbon atoms carries electrons, the π\piπ electrons, that move along the whole chain. Its bonds are drawn as alternating single and double bonds, but they are of nearly equal length. Kuhn modelled these electrons in 1949 as free particles in a box of the chain’s length LLL. With 2K2K2K such electrons filling the lowest KKK levels, two to a level (Lecture 14), the least energy that the electrons can absorb lifts one of them from the level KKK to the level K+1K+1K+1, and it is EK+1−EK=(2K+1)E1E_{K+1}-E_K=(2K+1)E_1EK+1​−EK​=(2K+1)E1​. For K=5K=5K=5 and L=1.4 nmL=1.4\,\mathrm{nm}L=1.4nm the model predicts the absorption of light of wavelength about 590 nm590\,\mathrm{nm}590nm, which is yellow. Such dyes do absorb visible light, the longer chains at longer wavelengths. The model works less well for chains whose bond lengths alternate, such as those of the polyenes (Autschbach, 2007).

16.4 Why the energy needs a domain

The energies grow without bound, En=n2E1E_n=n^2E_1En​=n2E1​, and this is where the new difficulty comes from. In the coefficients of (16.5), an energy that multiplies each stationary wave unu_nun​ by EnE_nEn​ multiplies each amplitude cnc_ncn​ by EnE_nEn​. For some states the result is not square-summable, so it is the coefficient sequence of no vector of L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L). §16.7 gives such a state, with cn∝1/n2c_n\propto1/n^2cn​∝1/n2. The energy multiplies the unit vector unu_nun​ by EnE_nEn​, so it is not bounded. By the Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem of §15.1, it cannot be a symmetric operator defined on every vector.

Recall from Lecture 15 the operators with a domain (Definition 15.1), the adjoint A†\adj AA† of a densely defined operator AAA (Definition 15.2), and the symmetric and the self-adjoint operators (Definition 15.3). A symmetric operator AAA has D(A)⊆D(A†)\dom A\subseteq\dom{\adj A}D(A)⊆D(A†), with A†φ=Aφ\adj A\varphi=A\varphiA†φ=Aφ there, and it is self-adjoint when the two domains are equal.

Postulate 16.5 (Observables with an eigenbasis). The observables of a particle in the box are self-adjoint operators on L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L). Suppose an observable AAA has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors e1,e2,…e_1,e_2,\dotse1​,e2​,…, with Aek=akekAe_k=a_ke_kAek​=ak​ek​. Then its readings are the numbers aka_kak​, and the measurement postulate (Born’s and Lüders’ rules, Postulate 4.1) holds, with the projection EaE_aEa​ (not an energy) Eaψ=∑k: ak=a⟨ek,ψ⟩ ek,Prob⁡(a)=∥Eaψ∥2=∑k: ak=a∣⟨ek,ψ⟩∣2.\begin{equation} \label{eq:16:Ea} E_a\psi=\sum_{k:\,a_k=a}\inner{e_k}\psi\,e_k ,\qquad \Prob(a)=\norm{E_a\psi}^2=\sum_{k:\,a_k=a}\abs{\inner{e_k}\psi}^2 . \end{equation}Ea​ψ=k:ak​=a∑​⟨ek​,ψ⟩ek​,Prob(a)=∥Ea​ψ∥2=k:ak​=a∑​∣⟨ek​,ψ⟩∣2.(16.9)

By Parseval’s identity, the probabilities add to 111. This is where completeness matters. If the basis missed a direction, part of the probability would be lost.

The postulate asks for self-adjoint operators, not only symmetric ones, for two reasons that later lectures give. By the spectral theorem, which Lecture 20 cites, exactly the self-adjoint operators are integrals of spectral measures, the rules that give the probabilities of readings in intervals. The evolutions in time are strongly continuous unitary groups. Lecture 22 proves that every such group is generated by a self-adjoint operator divided by ℏ\hbarℏ, and only sketches the converse, that every self-adjoint operator divided by ℏ\hbarℏ generates such a group.

So the energy postulate determines the Hamiltonian. Its domain holds more than the twice continuously differentiable functions that vanish at the walls (§16.7), and Lecture 18 describes it by derivatives. 16.7 A state outside the domain

Some states lie outside the domain of HHH. Take the coefficients cn=90π2n2(n=1,2,3,… ).c_n=\frac{\sqrt{90}}{\pi^2n^2}\qquad(n=1,2,3,\dots).cn​=π2n290​​(n=1,2,3,…). The domain records a moment of the energy readings. For ψ∈D(H)\psi\in\dom Hψ∈D(H), Parseval’s identity gives ∥Hψ∥2=∑nEn2∣cn∣2=∑nEn2Prob⁡(En),\norm{H\psi}^2=\sum_nE_n^2\abs{c_n}^2=\sum_nE_n^2\Prob(E_n),∥Hψ∥2=n∑​En2​∣cn​∣2=n∑​En2​Prob(En​), the mean square of the energy readings. So a state lies in D(H)\dom HD(H) exactly when its energy readings have a finite mean square. For the state above the mean is finite and the mean square is not.

The rule alone is not self-adjoint. Symmetry alone does not make an observable. Physics texts often call an operator Hermitian when it is symmetric. In infinite dimension, symmetric and self-adjoint are different conditions. Integration by parts, as in the step on the rule, proves that Schrödinger’s rule on the twice continuously differentiable functions that vanish at the walls is symmetric. It is not self-adjoint there.

We show this by finding a vector of D(H)\dom HD(H) outside the domain of the rule. Let ggg be the function equal to 111 on [0,L/2][0,L/2][0,L/2] and to 000 on (L/2,L)(L/2,L)(L/2,L), and let ψ\psiψ have the coefficients cn=⟨un,g⟩/Enc_n=\inner{u_n}g/E_ncn​=⟨un​,g⟩/En​. By Parseval’s identity, ∑nEn2∣cn∣2=∥g∥2<∞,\sum_nE_n^2\abs{c_n}^2=\norm g^2<\infty ,n∑​En2​∣cn​∣2=∥g∥2<∞, so ψ\psiψ lies in D(H)\dom HD(H), and Hψ=gH\psi=gHψ=g. The second derivative of this function jumps at L/2L/2L/2. If ψ\psiψ had a twice continuously differentiable representative that vanishes at the walls, the step on the rule would give Hψ=−(ℏ2/2m) ψ′′H\psi=-(\hbar^2/2m)\,\psi''Hψ=−(ℏ2/2m)ψ′′, and HψH\psiHψ would have a continuous representative. If the rule were self-adjoint, it would obey the energy postulate, and by the uniqueness in Theorem 16.8 it would equal HHH. So it is not self-adjoint.

The particle in a box, as a quantum system

States: the rays of L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L), represented by the sequences of coefficients cn=⟨un,ψ⟩c_n=\inner{u_n}\psicn​=⟨un​,ψ⟩ with ∑n∣cn∣2=1\sum_n\abs{c_n}^2=1∑n​∣cn​∣2=1, up to a common phase (Postulate 16.2 and Theorem 16.3). Observables: self-adjoint operators, each with its domain (Postulate 16.5 and Definition 15.3). Energy: HHH multiplies cnc_ncn​ by En=ℏ2π2n2/2mL2E_n=\hbar^2\pi^2n^2/2mL^2En​=ℏ2π2n2/2mL2 on the vectors with ∑nn4∣cn∣2<∞\sum_nn^4\abs{c_n}^2<\infty∑n​n4∣cn​∣2<∞. It is self-adjoint, and it is the only self-adjoint operator that obeys the energy postulate (Postulate 16.6 and Theorem 16.8). Measurement: the energy reads EnE_nEn​ with probability ∣cn∣2\abs{c_n}^2∣cn​∣2, and the state afterwards is unu_nun​ (Postulate 16.5).

Summary

  • The open chain: with κ=ℏ2/2ma2\kappa=\hbar^2/2ma^2κ=ℏ2/2ma2, its levels 4κsin⁡2(nπ/2N)4\kappa\sin^2(n\pi/2N)4κsin2(nπ/2N) approach the box’s energies En=n2E1E_n=n^2E_1En​=n2E1​ at each fixed nnn as the sites come closer (Proposition 16.1).
  • The box Hamiltonian is self-adjoint on its domain, and its eigenvalues are the EnE_nEn​. It is the only self-adjoint operator that obeys the energy postulate (Theorem 16.8).
  • A state outside the domain: the domain holds the states whose energy readings have a finite mean square.
  • The rule alone is not self-adjoint: on the twice continuously differentiable functions that vanish at the walls, it is symmetric, and it is a proper restriction of HHH (§16.7).
