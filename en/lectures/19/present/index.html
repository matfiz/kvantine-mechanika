Lecture 19

The free particle: observables without eigenvalues

The Fourier transform builds the position, momentum and free energy on the line. These observables are self-adjoint and have no eigenvalues. For every unit vector in S(R)\mathcal S(\R)S(R), we prove uncertainty with equality exactly for the Gaussian packets. In a box the same inequality fails for a self-adjoint momentum PθP_\thetaPθ​, at its eigenvectors.

The physics

De Broglie proposed in 1924 that a particle of momentum ppp has the wavelength h/ph/ph/p, with h=2πℏh=2\pi\hbarh=2πℏ. A packet whose momentum density is concentrated near p0p_0p0​ oscillates as eip0x/ℏe^{\ii p_0x/\hbar}eip0​x/ℏ, with this period. In 1999 Arndt, Nairz, Vos-Andreae, Keller, van der Zouw and Zeilinger sent molecules of C60_{60}60​, of mass 720 720\,720u, through a grating of period 100 100\,100nm at about 220 220\,220m/s. Their wavelength is about 2.5 2.5\,2.5pm, some 400400400 times smaller than the molecule’s diameter. So the first diffraction maxima lie about 25 μ25\,\mu25μrad from the beam, and the experiment resolved them.

Summary

  • The transform of a derivative: on S(R)\mathcal S(\R)S(R), the transform of −iℏψ′-\ii\hbar\psi'−iℏψ′ is pψ~p\fourier\psipψ~​ (Proposition 19.3), and Plancherel makes the transform unitary (Theorem 19.2).
  • Position, momentum and free energy are self-adjoint, none has an eigenvalue, and H0=P2/2mH_0=P^2/2mH0​=P2/2m (Theorem 19.6).
  • The canonical commutation relation holds on S(R)\mathcal S(\R)S(R) and on D(QP)∩D(PQ)\dom{QP}\cap\dom{PQ}D(QP)∩D(PQ) (Proposition 19.7).
  • The uncertainty relation: ΔQ ΔP≥ℏ/2\Delta Q\,\Delta P\ge\hbar/2ΔQΔP≥ℏ/2 for unit ψ∈S(R)\psi\in\mathcal S(\R)ψ∈S(R), with equality exactly for the Gaussian packets (Theorem 19.9), and on D(Q)∩D(P)\dom Q\cap\dom PD(Q)∩D(P) by a cited theorem (Theorem 19.10). In a box, the boundary term breaks it for PθP_\thetaPθ​, at its eigenvectors, and it holds on D(PD)\dom{P_D}D(PD​) (Proposition 19.11).
  • The momentum distribution of a Gaussian packet is normal, with the spread ℏ/2s\hbar/2sℏ/2s (Example 19.12).

His Prop. 12.11 solves this equation, with the derivative taken in a weaker sense. Hall, §12.2, gives the same example of the box as Proposition 19.11. Hall, Prop. A.22, states the integral GGG of Example 19.12.
